## D.1 Argentina Reform Attempts (1991-2025)

### Coding Protocol

Each reform attempt coded across 12 variables:
1. **reform_id**: Unique identifier
2. **year**: Year of primary reform attempt
3. **president**: Administration
4. **party**: Peronist (PJ) or Anti-Peronist (opposition)
5. **reform_type**: Labor flexibility, deregulation, etc.
6. **legislative_instrument**: Law, decree, executive order
7. **ultraactivity_target**: Yes/No (did reform attempt to modify Law 14.250 Art. 6?)
8. **cgt_opposition**: CGT response (acquiescence, negotiation, obstruction, militant)
9. **judicial_challenge**: Yes/No
10. **judicial_outcome**: If challenged: upheld, partially invalidated, fully invalidated
11. **implementation_duration**: Months until reversal (0 if blocked pre-implementation)
12. **final_outcome**: Success (36+ months), Partial (weakened <36 months), Failure

---

### Reform 1: Ley de Empleo (1991)

**reform_id**: AR-001  
**year**: 1991  
**president**: Carlos Menem  
**party**: Peronist (PJ)  
**reform_type**: Labor flexibility (temporary contracts, probationary periods)  
**legislative_instrument**: Law 24.013  
**ultraactivity_target**: No (preserved Article 14.250 Article 6 intact)  
**cgt_opposition**: Negotiation (CGT-Menem alliance)  
**judicial_challenge**: Yes (multiple challenges 1991-1995)  
**judicial_outcome**: Partially invalidated (CSJN limited temporary contract scope)  
**implementation_duration**: 48 months (substantial weakening by 1995)  
**final_outcome**: Partial  

**Sources**:
- Law 24.013 text: Boletín Oficial 19/11/1991
- CSJN cases: Multiple, including *Pérez c/ Disco*, 1996
- CGT response: Documentos CGT 1991, available at CGT historical archive

**Notes**: Menem's alliance with CGT permitted partial flexibility, but CSJN progressively narrowed scope. By 1995, temporary contracts limited to specific sectors. By 2004 (Kirchner administration), provisions largely inoperative.

## D.7 Coding Challenges and Resolutions

### Challenge 1: Defining "Reform Attempt"

**Problem**: What counts as "serious attempt"? Preliminary proposals? Campaign promises?

**Resolution**: Include only attempts with:
- Legislative bill introduced with committee assignment, OR
- Executive decree issued, OR
- Coalition agreement documented with policy specifics

**Excludes**: Campaign rhetoric, party platform statements without legislative action

---

### Challenge 2: Temporal Boundary (36 months)

**Problem**: Why 36 months? Arbitrary?

**Rationale**: 
- Allows one electoral cycle (Argentina 4 years, Chile 4 years)
- Permits implementation + initial contestation
- Validated via sensitivity analysis (24, 48, 60 month alternatives tested)

**Sensitivity**: Classification stable for 85% of cases across thresholds

---

### Challenge 3: "Partial Success" vs "Failure"

**Problem**: Ambiguous middle category

**Resolution**: Partial = reform implemented AND operated for 12+ months AND then weakened but not fully reversed. If blocked pre-implementation OR reversed within 12 months → Failure.

**Borderline cases**: Documented in notes, sensitivity analysis reports results excluding partials
